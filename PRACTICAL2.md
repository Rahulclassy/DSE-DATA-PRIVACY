Practical 2: Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) of a Smart Attendance System
Aim

To conduct a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) of a smart attendance system and identify potential privacy risks associated with the collection, processing, storage, and protection of students’ personal data.

Technology Selected
System: Smart Attendance System
Technology Used: Facial Recognition

Requirements
Computer or laptop

Internet connection

Web browser

Basic knowledge of privacy and data protection

Theory

A Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) is a structured process used to identify and evaluate the privacy risks associated with a new technology, application, or information system. It helps an organization understand how personal information will be collected, processed, stored, and shared, and allows suitable measures to be introduced to reduce privacy risks.

In this practical, a Smart Attendance System using facial recognition technology is considered. The system identifies students using their facial features and automatically records their attendance. Since facial recognition involves the processing of biometric information, protecting this data is an important privacy consideration.


1. Data Collection

The system may collect the following information:

Student name and identification number

Facial images

Biometric facial templates

Date and time of attendance

Classroom or location information

Only the information necessary for maintaining attendance should be collected.

2. Purpose of Data Processing

The collected information may be used to:

Identify students

Record attendance automatically

Maintain attendance records

Generate attendance reports

Reduce fraudulent attendance

The purpose of collecting the information should be clearly communicated to students.

3. Privacy Risks

The major privacy risks associated with the system include:

Unauthorized access to biometric information

Theft or leakage of facial data

Misuse of students' personal information

Excessive collection of personal data

Incorrect identification of students

Retention of biometric data for an unnecessary period

Unauthorized sharing of information with third parties

4. Risk Assessment

Risk	Impact
Biometric data breach	High
Unauthorized access	High
Unauthorized data sharing	High
Incorrect identification	Medium
Excessive data collection	Medium
Excessive data retention	Medium

5. Privacy Protection Measures

The following measures can be implemented to reduce privacy risks:

Collect only the minimum information required.

Encrypt biometric information during storage and transmission.

Restrict access to authorized personnel.

Use strong passwords and authentication mechanisms.

Establish a clear data retention and deletion policy.

Regularly audit and test the system for security weaknesses.

Inform students about the purpose of data collection.

Provide an alternative attendance method where appropriate.

Ensure that inaccurate attendance records can be corrected.

6. Data Retention

Biometric information should not be stored permanently without a valid reason. A specific retention period should be established, and data should be securely deleted when it is no longer required.

7. Transparency and User Rights

Students should be informed about:

What information is being collected

Why the information is being collected

How the information is protected

Who can access the information

How long the information will be stored

How they can raise privacy concerns or request correction of inaccurate information

Result

The Privacy Impact Assessment of the Smart Attendance System was successfully conducted. The assessment identified potential privacy risks related to biometric data collection, storage, access, retention, and sharing, along with appropriate measures for mitigating these risks.

Conclusion

A Privacy Impact Assessment helps organizations identify and manage privacy risks before implementing a new technology or system. In a facial-recognition-based attendance system, particular attention should be given to data minimization, biometric data protection, access control, retention, transparency, and user rights. Proper privacy safeguards can reduce potential risks while allowing the system to perform its intended purpose.
