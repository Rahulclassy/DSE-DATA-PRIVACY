Practical 1: Data Privacy Audit of Amazon


Aim

To conduct a data privacy audit of the Amazon website and identify how user data is collected, used, stored, shared, and protected.

Website Selected
Organization: Amazon
Website: Amazon
Privacy Policy: Amazon Privacy Notice

Requirements
Internet connection

Web browser

Amazon account (optional)

Theory

A Data Privacy Audit is a systematic examination of an organization's practices for collecting, processing, storing, sharing, and protecting personal information. The purpose of the audit is to identify possible privacy risks and determine whether appropriate privacy and security measures are being followed.

For this practical, Amazon is selected as the organization. An e-commerce website handles different types of user information while providing services such as account management, product purchases, payments, delivery, and personalized recommendations.

During the audit, the following areas are examined:

1. Data Collection

Amazon may collect information such as:

Name and contact details

Account and login information

Delivery and billing addresses

Payment-related information

Order and purchase history

Search and browsing activity

Device and technical information

Information provided when contacting customer service

2. Data Usage

The collected information may be used for purposes such as:

Processing and delivering orders

Providing customer support

Managing user accounts

Processing payments

Improving website and services

Personalizing recommendations and advertisements

Detecting fraud and maintaining security

3. Cookies and Tracking

Websites may use cookies and similar technologies to remember user preferences, maintain sessions, understand website usage, and support personalized content or advertising.

Users should review available cookie and privacy settings to understand and manage applicable choices.

4. Third-Party Sharing

An organization may share certain information with service providers and other third parties when required to provide services, process transactions, deliver products, support advertising, maintain security, or comply with legal requirements.

5. Data Storage and Protection

Organizations handling personal information should use appropriate technical and organizational safeguards to protect data against unauthorized access, loss, misuse, or disclosure. Examples include access controls, encryption, authentication, monitoring, and security procedures.

6. Privacy Risks

Possible privacy risks that can be examined during the audit include:

Collection of excessive personal information

Long-term retention of user data

Tracking through cookies and similar technologies

Sharing information with third-party service providers

Unauthorized access or data breaches

Users not fully understanding how their information is processed

7. Privacy Controls

Users can reduce privacy risks by:

Using strong and unique passwords

Enabling multi-factor authentication where available

Reviewing account privacy and security settings

Managing cookie and advertising preferences

Avoiding unnecessary personal information sharing

Reading the organization's privacy notice

Conclusion

A data privacy audit helps determine how an organization handles personal information throughout its lifecycle. In the case of Amazon, the audit focuses on data collection, usage, cookies and tracking, third-party sharing, security measures, and potential privacy risks. Such an audit helps users understand how their information may be handled and encourages organizations to maintain responsible data privacy practices.
